Statistics & Probability · SP.2a The probability scale

Demonstrate understanding that probability is a measure, on a scale from 0 to 1, of how likely an event is to occur.

\(P(E)=0\)

Impossible

\(0<P(E)<1\)

Possible

\(P(E)=1\)

Certain

Statistics & Probability · SP.2b Equally likely outcomes

For equally likely outcomes, probability is the number of outcomes of interest divided by the total number of outcomes.

\[ P(E) = \frac{ \text{number of outcomes of interest} }{ \text{total number of outcomes} } \]
Roll a fair die Probability of an even number: \[ P(\text{even}) = \frac{3}{6} = \frac12 \]
Statistics & Probability · SP.2c Relative frequency

Use relative frequency as an estimate of the probability of an event, given experimental data.

\[ \text{relative frequency} = \frac{ \text{number of times event occurs} }{ \text{number of trials} } \]
Experiment Heads occurs 47 times in 100 tosses. \[ P(H)\approx\frac{47}{100}=0.47 \]
Statistics & Probability · SP.2c Repeat the experiment

Increasing the number of times an experiment is repeated generally leads to progressively better estimates of its theoretical probability.

10 trials estimate may vary greatly
100 trials usually steadier
1000 trials typically closer to theoretical probability
Important More trials improve the estimate; they do not force the experimental result to equal the theoretical value.

Statistics & Probability · SP.3e Describe the centre

Select, calculate and interpret appropriate summary statistics to describe aspects of univariate data.

Mean \[ \bar{x} = \frac{\sum x}{n} \]
Median

Middle value when the observations are ordered.

Mode

Most frequently occurring value or category.

Statistics & Probability · SP.3e Describe the variability

The specified measure of variability is the range.

\[ \text{Range} = \text{maximum} - \text{minimum} \]

Data: \(3,\ 5,\ 5,\ 8,\ 12\)

\[ \text{Range}=12-3=9 \]

A measure of centre and a measure of spread answer different questions about the same data.

Statistics & Probability · SP.3e Mean from a frequency distribution

The learning outcome includes the mean of a grouped frequency distribution.

\[ \bar{x} \approx \frac{ \sum ( \text{class midpoint} \times \text{frequency} ) }{ \sum \text{frequency} } \]

Because the individual observations inside each interval are not known, the class midpoint is used to represent the values in that interval.

Statistics & Probability · SP.3f Evaluate the display

Evaluate the effectiveness of different graphical displays in representing data.

What is visible? pattern · centre · spread · comparison
What is hidden? individual values · scale · sample size
Is it appropriate? Does the display suit the data?
Statistics & Probability · SP.3g Misuse of statistics

Discuss misconceptions and misuses of statistics.

Truncated scale

A graph can exaggerate a small difference.

Unrepresentative sample

A large sample can still be biased.

Misleading summary

One statistic may hide important features of a distribution.

Ask: What data were collected, from whom, and how?
