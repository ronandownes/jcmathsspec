Appendix B · Geometry for Post-primary School Mathematics The geometry reference

Appendix B sets out the agreed system of geometry for post-primary mathematics and was updated for the 2018 Junior Cycle Mathematics Specification.

5 core axioms
21 numbered theorems
6 numbered corollaries
22 prescribed constructions
Purpose To give definitions, axioms and previously established results a logical order so that geometrical arguments and proofs have a common foundation.
Appendix B · Sections 1–4 Three levels of geometry
Level 1

Fully rigorous mathematical treatment.

Level 2

Semi-formal geometry suitable from roughly age 14 upwards.

Level 3

Informal geometry suitable for younger learners.

Appendix B · The Theory Axioms 1–5
Axiom 1 · Two Points

Exactly one line passes through any two given points.

Axiom 2 · Ruler

Establishes the basic properties of distance and marking a specified distance on a ray.

Axiom 3 · Protractor

Establishes degree measure and the construction and addition of angle measures.

Axiom 4 · SAS + ASA + SSS

Gives the stated conditions under which two triangles are congruent.

Axiom 5 · Parallels

Through a given point there is exactly one line parallel to a given line.

Appendix B · Theorems Theorems 1–6
Theorem 1

Vertically opposite angles are equal.

Theorem 2

In an isosceles triangle, angles opposite equal sides are equal; the converse also holds.

Theorem 3

Equal alternate angles imply parallel lines; parallel lines imply equal alternate angles.

Theorem 4

The angles in a triangle add to 180°.

Theorem 5

Two lines are parallel if and only if corresponding angles made by a transversal are equal.

Theorem 6

An exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles.

Appendix B · Theorems Theorems 7–12
Theorem 7

The greater side of a triangle is opposite the greater angle; the converse also holds.

Theorem 8

Any two sides of a triangle are together greater than the third side.

Theorem 9

Opposite sides and opposite angles of a parallelogram are equal.

Corollary 1

A diagonal divides a parallelogram into two congruent triangles.

Theorem 10

The diagonals of a parallelogram bisect one another.

Theorems 11 & 12

Parallel lines establish equal divisions and proportional divisions on transversals and triangle sides.

Appendix B · Theorems Theorems 13–18
Theorem 13

Corresponding sides of similar triangles are proportional.

Theorem 14 · Pythagoras

In a right-angled triangle, a² + b² = c².

Theorem 15

Converse to Pythagoras: if a² + b² = c², the relevant angle is right.

Theorem 16

For a triangle, base × height does not depend on which side is selected as base.

Theorem 17

A diagonal of a parallelogram bisects its area.

Theorem 18

Area of a parallelogram = base × height.

Appendix B · Circles Theorems 19–21 & corollaries
Theorem 19

The angle at the centre standing on an arc is twice the angle at the circumference standing on the same arc.

Corollaries 2–5

Same-arc angles; angle in a semicircle; diameter converse; cyclic quadrilateral opposite angles.

Theorem 20

A tangent is perpendicular to the radius at its point of contact, and conversely.

Corollary 6

Two circles sharing a common tangent at one point have their centres and that point collinear.

Theorem 21

A perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord; its perpendicular bisector passes through the centre.

Appendix B · Section 8 Discovery before formal proof
Investigate examples and diagrams
→
Notice apparently invariant features
→
Conjecture possible general result
→
Justify reason and prove

The geometry results are intended to be encountered first through investigation and discovery. Students then move from evidence based on examples towards increasingly convincing logical arguments.

Key distinction Many examples can suggest that a statement is true; a proof establishes why the result must hold.
