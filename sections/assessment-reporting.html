Assessment & Reporting · p.22 Why Classroom-Based Assessments?

The Classroom-Based Assessments link to the priorities for learning and teaching in mathematics, with particular emphasis on problem solving and communicating.

Authentic

Students actively engage in practical and authentic mathematical learning experiences.

Mathematical proficiency

Students develop and demonstrate mathematical knowledge, reasoning and communication.

Common level

All students complete both Classroom-Based Assessments at a common level.

Teacher judgement Attainment is considered through Subject Learning and Assessment Review and school reporting to parents and students.
Assessment & Reporting · p.22 CBA 1 · Mathematical Investigation
Format

Report which may be presented in a wide range of formats.

Duration

Students investigate a mathematical problem over a three-week period.

Completion

End of second year followed by one review meeting.

PROBLEM-SOLVING CYCLE

DEFINE DECOMPOSE SIMPLIFY MATHEMATISE SOLVE INTERPRET

Students define a problem, decompose it into manageable parts and/or simplify it using appropriate assumptions, translate it to mathematics where necessary, engage with and solve the problem if possible, and interpret findings in the context of the original problem.

Assessment & Reporting · p.23 CBA 2 · Statistical Investigation
Format

Report which may be presented in a wide range of formats.

Duration

Students follow the statistical enquiry cycle over a three-week period.

Completion

End of first term of third year followed by one review meeting.

STATISTICAL ENQUIRY CYCLE

QUESTION PLAN COLLECT ORGANISE ANALYSE ANSWER

Students formulate a question; plan and collect unbiased, representative data; organise and manage the data; explore and analyse it using appropriate displays and numerical summaries; and answer the original question giving reasons based on the analysis.

Assessment & Reporting · p.24 Inclusive assessment practices

The specification allows for inclusive assessment practices in ongoing assessment and Classroom-Based Assessments.

Need

A student may have a specific physical or learning difficulty.

Accommodation

Reasonable accommodations may reduce, as far as possible, the impact of the difficulty on performance.

Consistency

Supports should be in line with arrangements already used to support the student's learning.

Examples Support may include assistance from a special needs assistant or assistive technologies where appropriate.
